Conversion of Internal Coordinates to Cartesian Coordinates

Formulae

Atom 1 p1 = (0,  0,  0)
Atom 2 p2 = (r12,  0,  0)
Atom 3 p3 = (r12 − r23 cos θ123,  r23 sin θ123,  0)
Atom 4
e1 = (p3 − p2) / |p3 − p2|
e3 = ((p2 − p1) × e1) / |(p2 − p1) × e1|
e2 = e3 × e1
M = [ e1   e2   e3 ]
p4  =  p3  +  M  ·  [
−r34 cos θ234
r34 sin θ234 cos φ1234
r34 sin θ234 sin φ1234
]

Variables

Symbol Meaning Units
p1, p2, p3, p4 Cartesian position vectors of atoms 1 to 4. Atom 1 has no internal coordinates given, so it is taken as the origin, and the molecule’s overall position and orientation are arbitrary. Å
r12 Bond length between atoms 1 and 2. Å
r23 Bond length between atoms 2 and 3. Å
r34 Bond length between atoms 3 and 4. Å
θ123 Bond angle at atom 2, between the bonds 2–1 and 2–3. It is the interior angle at atom 2, which is why cos θ123 enters with a minus sign. degrees
θ234 Bond angle at atom 3, between the bonds 3–2 and 3–4. degrees
φ1234 Torsion (dihedral) angle about the 2–3 bond: the angle between the plane through atoms 1, 2, 3 and the plane through atoms 2, 3, 4. φ = 0 is the eclipsed (cis) arrangement; positive values rotate clockwise when looking from atom 2 toward atom 3. degrees
e1 Unit vector along the 2→3 bond, i.e. the bond being extended. Local x axis. —
e3 Unit vector normal to the plane through atoms 1, 2, 3. This is the axis that φ1234 rotates about. Local z axis. —
e2 Unit vector completing the right-handed set, e3 × e1. Local y axis. —
M 3×3 matrix holding e1, e2, e3 as its columns. Because e1 × e2 = e3 it is a pure rotation, so it carries the bracketed vector from the local frame into world coordinates without changing its length. —
× Vector cross product. —
| v | Euclidean length of the vector v, used to normalise to unit length. —
[ … ] The position of atom 4 relative to atom 3, written in spherical polar form in the local e1e2e3 frame. Its length is r34. Å
